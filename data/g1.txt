은/는|topic particle: "as for…"|Noun ending in a consonant + 은; noun ending in a vowel + 는|Marks what the sentence is about, and is also used for contrast. Compare 이/가, which marks a new or focused subject.|저는 학생이에요.=I am a student.|오늘은 바빠요. 내일은 괜찮아요.=Today I'm busy. Tomorrow is fine.
이/가|subject particle|Noun ending in a consonant + 이; noun ending in a vowel + 가|Marks the subject, especially new information or the answer to "who/what". Always used before 있다/없다, 아니다 and 되다. 저+가 → 제가, 나+가 → 내가, 누구+가 → 누가.|날씨가 좋아요.=The weather is nice.|누가 왔어요? 친구가 왔어요.=Who came? My friend came.
을/를|object particle|Noun ending in a consonant + 을; noun ending in a vowel + 를|Marks the direct object of an action verb. Often dropped in casual speech.|저는 책을 읽어요.=I read a book.|어제 영화를 봤어요.=I watched a movie yesterday.
에|"at / on / in" (time); "to" (destination); "at / in" (location with 있다/없다)|Noun + 에|Used for a point in time, a destination with 가다/오다, or where something exists. Not used after 오늘, 내일, 어제, 지금.|저는 7시에 일어나요.=I get up at 7 o'clock.|주말에 도서관에 가요.=I go to the library on the weekend.
에서|"at / in" (place where an action happens); "from" (starting place)|Noun + 에서|Use 에서 when an action takes place somewhere; use 에 when something simply exists there.|저는 회사에서 일해요.=I work at a company.|말레이시아에서 왔어요.=I came from Malaysia.
도|"also, too"|Noun + 도 (replaces 은/는, 이/가, 을/를)|Adds the noun to something already mentioned. It can attach after other particles: 에도, 에서도.|저도 커피를 좋아해요.=I like coffee too.|사과를 샀어요. 바나나도 샀어요.=I bought apples. I bought bananas too.
-아요/어요|polite present tense ending|Stem whose last vowel is ㅏ or ㅗ + 아요; other stems + 어요; 하다 → 해요|The everyday polite style, used for statements, questions, suggestions and soft commands depending on intonation. Vowels contract: 가+아요 → 가요, 마시+어요 → 마셔요.|저는 매일 한국어를 공부해요.=I study Korean every day.|지금 뭐 먹어요?=What are you eating now?
-았어요/었어요|polite past tense ending|Stem with ㅏ or ㅗ + 았어요; other stems + 었어요; 하다 → 했어요|Expresses a completed action or past state.|어제 친구를 만났어요.=I met a friend yesterday.|주말에 집에서 쉬었어요.=I rested at home on the weekend.
-(으)ㄹ 거예요|future / intention: "will, be going to"; also a guess: "probably"|Stem ending in a consonant + 을 거예요; stem ending in a vowel or ㄹ + ㄹ 거예요|With a first-person subject it states a plan; with a third-person subject it often expresses a guess.|내일 친구를 만날 거예요.=I'm going to meet a friend tomorrow.|주말에는 비가 올 거예요.=It will probably rain on the weekend.
-고 싶다|"want to"|Verb stem + 고 싶다|Used for the speaker's own wish, or to ask the listener's. For a third person, use -고 싶어 하다.|한국에 가고 싶어요.=I want to go to Korea.|뭐 먹고 싶어요?=What do you want to eat?
안 / -지 않다|negation: "not"|안 + verb/adjective; or stem + 지 않다. For 하다 verbs made from a noun: noun + 안 하다 (공부 안 해요).|안 is short and common in speech; -지 않다 is slightly more formal. Use 못 instead when you mean "cannot".|오늘은 학교에 안 가요.=I'm not going to school today.|이 음식은 맵지 않아요.=This food is not spicy.
-(으)세요|polite request or command: "please do"|Stem ending in a consonant + 으세요; stem ending in a vowel or ㄹ + 세요 (ㄹ drops: 만들다 → 만드세요)|Used to politely tell or ask someone to do something. The negative is -지 마세요, "please don't".|여기에 앉으세요.=Please sit here.|교실에서 떠들지 마세요.=Please don't make noise in the classroom.
-고|"and" (listing or sequence)|Stem + 고|Connects two clauses, either listing facts or showing that one action follows another. Tense is usually marked only on the final verb.|이 식당은 싸고 맛있어요.=This restaurant is cheap and delicious.|숙제를 하고 텔레비전을 봤어요.=I did my homework and then watched TV.
-아서/어서|"so, because"; also "and then" (closely linked actions)|Stem with ㅏ or ㅗ + 아서; other stems + 어서; 하다 → 해서|Gives a reason or links two connected actions. It cannot take a past-tense marker, and the following clause cannot be a command or suggestion (use -(으)니까 for that).|배가 아파서 병원에 갔어요.=My stomach hurt, so I went to the hospital.|친구를 만나서 같이 영화를 봤어요.=I met my friend and we watched a movie together.
-지만|"but, although"|Stem + 지만; past: -았/었지만|Connects two contrasting clauses.|한국어는 어렵지만 재미있어요.=Korean is difficult but fun.|비가 왔지만 운동을 했어요.=It rained, but I exercised.
