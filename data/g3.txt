-(으)ㄴ/는 것 같다|"it seems that, I think that"|Verb present: stem + 는 것 같다; verb past and adjective present: stem + (으)ㄴ 것 같다; future/guess: stem + (으)ㄹ 것 같다|Expresses a guess, or softens the speaker's own opinion so it sounds less direct.|밖에 비가 오는 것 같아요.=It seems to be raining outside.|이 옷은 저한테 좀 큰 것 같아요.=I think these clothes are a bit big for me.
-게 되다|"come to, end up doing"|Stem + 게 되다|Shows a change of situation that came about through circumstances rather than the subject's own will.|회사 일 때문에 한국에 오게 됐어요.=I ended up coming to Korea because of work.|처음에는 못 먹었는데 이제 김치를 좋아하게 됐어요.=At first I couldn't eat it, but now I've come to like kimchi.
-다가|"while doing… (then)"; interruption or change of action|Verb stem + 다가|The first action is interrupted or shifts to another. Both clauses share the same subject.|집에 가다가 친구를 만났어요.=I ran into a friend on my way home.|영화를 보다가 잠이 들었어요.=I fell asleep while watching the movie.
-도록|"so that"; "to the point that"|Stem + 도록|Expresses the purpose or goal of the following action, or the extent it reaches. -도록 하다 means "make sure to".|잊어버리지 않도록 메모하세요.=Take a note so that you don't forget.|내일은 늦지 않도록 하겠습니다.=I'll make sure I'm not late tomorrow.
-(으)ㄹ수록|"the more…, the more…"|Stem ending in a consonant + 을수록; stem ending in a vowel or ㄹ + ㄹ수록|Often used together with -(으)면: 보면 볼수록.|한국어는 공부할수록 재미있어요.=The more I study Korean, the more fun it is.|이 노래는 들으면 들을수록 좋아요.=The more I listen to this song, the more I like it.
-자마자|"as soon as"|Verb stem + 자마자|The second action happens immediately after the first.|집에 도착하자마자 샤워를 했어요.=I took a shower as soon as I got home.|수업이 끝나자마자 식당으로 뛰어갔어요.=I ran to the cafeteria as soon as class ended.
-는 바람에|"because (something unexpected happened)" — negative result|Verb stem + 는 바람에|Used when an unexpected event causes an unwanted result. The main clause is in the past tense and is never a command or suggestion.|버스를 놓치는 바람에 회사에 늦었어요.=I missed the bus, so I was late for work.|갑자기 비가 오는 바람에 옷이 다 젖었어요.=It suddenly rained, so my clothes got completely wet.
-(으)ㄴ/는 편이다|"tend to, be on the … side"|Verb stem + 는 편이다; adjective stem + (으)ㄴ 편이다|Describes a general tendency rather than an absolute fact.|저는 아침에 일찍 일어나는 편이에요.=I tend to get up early in the morning.|우리 동네는 조용한 편이에요.=My neighborhood is on the quiet side.
-던 / -았던/었던|"that (I) used to… / that was once…" (recollection)|Stem + 던 (repeated or unfinished past action); stem + 았던/었던 (completed past action or state)|A noun-modifying form used when recalling the past.|이 노래는 제가 고등학교 때 자주 듣던 노래예요.=This is a song I used to listen to often in high school.|지난번에 갔던 식당에 다시 가고 싶어요.=I want to go again to the restaurant we went to last time.
-(으)ㄹ 뿐만 아니라|"not only… but also"|Stem ending in a consonant + 을 뿐만 아니라; stem ending in a vowel or ㄹ + ㄹ 뿐만 아니라; noun + 뿐만 아니라|Adds a second fact pointing in the same direction (both positive or both negative).|그 가수는 노래를 잘할 뿐만 아니라 춤도 잘 춰요.=That singer not only sings well but also dances well.|이 식당은 음식뿐만 아니라 분위기도 좋아요.=At this restaurant not only the food but the atmosphere is good too.
-느라고|"because of doing… (so I couldn't / it was hard)"|Verb stem + 느라고|The first action took up time or effort and caused a negative result. Same subject in both clauses; no past-tense marker before 느라고.|숙제를 하느라고 잠을 못 잤어요.=I couldn't sleep because I was doing my homework.|이사 준비를 하느라고 요즘 바빠요.=I've been busy lately getting ready to move.
-(으)려면|"if you want to / intend to…"|Verb stem ending in a consonant + 으려면; stem ending in a vowel or ㄹ + 려면|Short for -(으)려고 하면. The following clause states what is required or advised.|한국어를 잘하려면 매일 연습해야 해요.=If you want to be good at Korean, you have to practice every day.|시청에 가려면 몇 번 버스를 타야 돼요?=Which bus do I have to take to get to City Hall?
-다고 하다|indirect (reported) speech: "(someone) says that…"|Verb: stem + ㄴ/는다고 하다; adjective: stem + 다고 하다; noun: (이)라고 하다; past: -았/었다고 하다. Question: -냐고 하다. Command: -(으)라고 하다. Suggestion: -자고 하다.|Reports what someone said without quoting directly. Often contracted in speech: -대요, -냬요, -래요, -재요.|친구가 내일 한국에 온다고 했어요.=My friend said he is coming to Korea tomorrow.|의사 선생님이 푹 쉬라고 하셨어요.=The doctor told me to rest well.
-아/어 버리다|"end up doing; do completely"|Stem with ㅏ or ㅗ + 아 버리다; other stems + 어 버리다|Stresses that an action is completely finished, often with a feeling of relief or regret.|남은 음식을 다 먹어 버렸어요.=I ate up all the leftover food.|약속을 잊어버려서 친구가 화가 났어요.=I completely forgot our appointment, so my friend got angry.
-(으)ㄹ 텐데|"I expect that…, so / but…"|Stem ending in a consonant + 을 텐데; stem ending in a vowel or ㄹ + ㄹ 텐데|States the speaker's expectation or assumption as background for a suggestion, worry or contrast.|길이 막힐 텐데 지하철을 탑시다.=The roads will probably be congested, so let's take the subway.|피곤할 텐데 일찍 들어가서 쉬세요.=You must be tired, so go home early and rest.
